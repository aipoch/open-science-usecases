# How much does battery health decline after two years of smartphone use

## Research question

What can selected owner reports say about one model’s battery health after roughly two years?

Summarizes self-reported battery-health observations for a selected phone model after roughly two years. The archive records selection criteria, descriptive statistics, and source discussions.

## Data and comparison design

Retain only reports identifying the phone model, an apparent original battery, and the defined use-duration range. Summarize the distribution rather than presenting one impressive or disappointing report as typical.

## Recorded findings

The 21-report convenience sample suggests an iPhone 14 Pro Max typically retains **about 87% battery health after roughly two years**, equivalent to **13 percentage points of degradation**.

| Statistic | Battery health | Degradation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean | 86.8% | 13.2 points |
| Median | 86% | 14 points |
| Interquartile range | 85–88% | 12–15 points |
| Observed range | 81–94% | 6–19 points |
| Standard deviation | 3.7 points | 3.7 points |

Distribution:

- 80–84% health: 4/21 reports (19%)
- 85–89% health: 13/21 (62%)
- 90–94% health: 4/21 (19%)
- Below 80% or above 94%: none

The reports came from relevant [Reddit discussions](https://www.reddit.com/r/iPhone14Pro/comments/1i78qjp/14_pro_max_going_2_years_strong/) and [MacRumors owner threads](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/iphone-14-pro-max-battery-health-after-a-year.2398400/page-17). Only reports identifying the 14 Pro Max, an apparent original battery, and 18–30 months of use were retained.

This is an exploratory convenience sample, not a representative survey. Battery health was self-reported and not independently tested; ages were sometimes inferred from purchase dates; users with unusually good or bad batteries may be more likely to post; and multiple reports could conceivably come from the same person. Apple’s displayed health is also a rounded software estimate. The defensible conclusion is therefore that **mid-to-high 80s appears common after two years**, not that 86.8% is a precise population average.

## What the comparison shows

The convenience sample is self-reported and may contain selection bias or uncertain ages. Percentage-point loss is not the same as a measured decline in everyday runtime.

## Assumptions and limits

These owner reports form a convenience sample, not a representative degradation study. Device history, reporting selection, and the battery-health estimate limit generalization to other phones.

# Spending $100 on Ads vs. $100 on Content Which

## Research question

What changes when a marketing comparison uses immediate response versus a mature program?

Examines archived evidence about advertising and content-marketing efficiency and converts reported lead costs to a fixed-budget comparison. The record distinguishes immediate response from longer-term accumulation.

## Data and comparison design

Convert reported cost per lead into leads per $100 using 100 divided by the reported cost. Keep collection period, channel definition, and program maturity visible, and distinguish unlike studies rather than pooling them as a controlled experiment.

## Recorded findings

Based on the best available real-world data, **content is more likely to produce more leads per $100 over the long term, while advertising produces results faster**.

The closest direct comparison reported:

| Channel | Cost per lead | Leads per $100 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paid search | $108–$109 | 0.92 |
| Content marketing | $64–$75 | 1.33–1.56 |

Across 24 months, content therefore generated approximately **45%–70% more leads per dollar**. Once the content program matured, it reached about **3.1 leads per $100**, versus **0.9 from paid search**. [Kapost/Eloqua study](https://www.social4retail.com/uploads/1/0/9/8/10981970/_________contentmarketingkaposteloquaebook_1.pdf)

Modern advertising data are more favorable to ads: more than 13,000 U.S. search campaigns produced an average cost per lead of $66.69 in 2026—approximately **1.5 leads per $100**. [WordStream/LocalIQ data](https://www.wordstream.com/ppc-benchmarks)

### Conclusion

If the objective is **immediate customer opportunities**, spend the $100 on ads.

If the objective is **maximum long-term return** and you can consistently fund content for many months, spend it on content. Available evidence suggests content eventually produces more leads per dollar because published material continues attracting prospects after its production cost has been paid.

However, the evidence measures leads—not confirmed customers. Therefore, the most accurate conclusion is:

> **Content likely wins on long-term lead efficiency; ads win on speed and predictability. The available real data do not establish which acquires more paying customers.**

## What the comparison shows

This is a synthesis of observational benchmarks, not a randomized decision rule for a particular business or a promise of returns from a one-off $100 spend.

## Assumptions and limits

The evidence concerns leads rather than confirmed customers and combines sources with different time horizons. It does not establish a causal return for a new $100 campaign or current advertising prices.

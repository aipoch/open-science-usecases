# For the same one-square-meter area, which receives more solar energy over the course of a year London or Seattle

## Research question

Can matched sunshine-duration records answer a question about annual solar energy?

Compares matched-period sunshine-duration normals for London and Seattle and exports the standardized annual totals. The session explains its conversion to a one-square-metre illustrative comparison.

## Data and comparison design

The comparison uses London–Gatwick and Seattle–Tacoma over the same 1961–1990 normal period, sum monthly sunshine duration, and label any conversion to a one-square-metre illustration. Do not treat hours of sunshine as measured global horizontal irradiation.

## Recorded findings

| City or contrast | Matched sunshine-duration result | What the unit means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| London | 1,574.2 hours/year | 1961–1990 sunshine-duration normal. |
| Seattle | 2,169.7 hours/year | The same type of historical normal. |
| Seattle minus London | 595.5 hours; 37.8% more | A duration comparison, not 37.8% more photovoltaic yield. |

The recorded annual totals are 1,574.2 hours for London and 2,169.7 for Seattle: a 595.5-hour, or 37.8%, difference.

## What the comparison shows

The station comparison supports the sunshine-duration contrast, not a measured energy yield for rooftops or solar panels.

## Assumptions and limits

Sunshine duration is a proxy, not measured global horizontal irradiation. The one-sun-equivalent calculation must not be interpreted as actual annual solar energy received by a panel.

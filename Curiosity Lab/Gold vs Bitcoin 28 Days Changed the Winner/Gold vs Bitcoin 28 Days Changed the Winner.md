# Gold vs Bitcoin 28 Days Changed the Winner

## Research question

How sensitive is an equal-dollar asset comparison to the historical entry checkpoint?

Values equal-dollar GLD and IBIT purchases using a supplied price table and compares several entry dates. The archived tables and charts illustrate how the winner changes with the chosen historical start.

## Data and comparison design

Convert the same dollar amount into holdings at each supplied entry price and value them at matched checkpoints. Keep GLD and IBIT identified as fund proxies, and compare each entry separately rather than blending different holding intervals.

## Recorded findings

Holding until Sep 23 from each available start date:

| Start date | GLD ends at | IBIT ends at | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-01-02 | $986.44 | $939.93 | **GLD** |
| 2026-01-30 | $882.98 | $1,008.21 | **IBIT** |
| 2026-02-27 | $812.16 | $1,287.44 | **IBIT** |
| 2026-03-31 | $913.06 | $1,246.23 | **IBIT** |

**How the start date changes things:**
- Jan 2 is the *only* start date where GLD wins, and even then barely ($46 on $1,000) — you're basically flat.
- Starting in February or March flips the winner to IBIT, often handsomely — buying IBIT right after its drawdown ($37 in late Feb) gave ~$1,287 by September.
- Every starting date loses money on GLD from Feb 27 onward, since late February was near its peak in this file.

With only four start dates and one end date, this shows the entry point matters a great deal at each fund's swing points — but it's a small sample of one year for a volatile pair, not a pattern to trade on.

## What the comparison shows

The supplied checkpoints are not independent investment experiments. ETF proxies, selected dates, and the common exit define the result.

## Assumptions and limits

The analysis concerns the supplied checkpoints and ETF proxies. Entry-date sensitivity is not a prediction of future returns or a recommendation to buy either asset.

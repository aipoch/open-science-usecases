# Can 20 Pokémon Packs Guarantee an SIR

## Research question

Does seeing frequent rare-card pulls make a short run of packs a guarantee?

Examines reported Special Illustration Rare pulls and illustrates the chance of opening a run of packs without a hit. The conversation distinguishes observed sample rates from an assumed per-pack probability.

## Data and comparison design

The analysis separates the reported livestream sample rate from the collector’s inferred underlying pull rate. For an assumed independent per-pack probability p, a run of n packs has no-hit probability (1-p)^n. The assumed probability and the sampling process must stay visible.

## Recorded findings

| Packs opened | Probability of no SIR at p=0.05 | Probability of at least one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 0.95^10 ≈ 59.9% | ≈40.1% |
| 20 | 0.95^20 ≈ 35.8% | ≈64.2% |
| 30 | 0.95^30 ≈ 21.5% | ≈78.5% |
| Expected count for 20 packs | 20 × 0.05 = 1 | An expectation of one is not a guarantee of one. |

The calculation assumes independent packs with a fixed per-pack success probability. If p is the SIR probability and n packs are opened, P(no SIR) = (1 − p)^n and P(at least one) = 1 − (1 − p)^n. The archive discusses a community livestream sample rather than official manufacturer odds. A sample estimate can be uncertain or unrepresentative without making a twenty-pack guarantee valid. At p = 0.05, the expected number is exactly one in twenty packs, while the probability of getting none is still about 36%. Claims that promotional packs were selected or that the social-media feed hides losses were speculative explanations, not demonstrated features of that source sample.

## What the comparison shows

Expected hits are not guaranteed hits. Social-media selection and promotional livestream product may both differ from ordinary retail packs; the example does not establish printed manufacturer odds.

## Assumptions and limits

The sample is not an official guarantee and may not represent all packs. The dry-streak calculation assumes independent packs with a constant probability; it does not ensure a hit after any fixed number of purchases.

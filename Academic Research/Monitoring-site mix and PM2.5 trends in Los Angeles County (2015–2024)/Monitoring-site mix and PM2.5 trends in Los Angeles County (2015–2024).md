# Monitoring-site mix and PM2.5 trends in Los Angeles County (2015–2024)

## Research question

Does the apparent county trend change when the same monitoring sites are followed throughout?

This case uses public EPA monitoring observations to examine how changing monitoring-site membership affects the apparent 2015–2024 PM2.5 trend in Los Angeles County. It compares the observed network with six sites observed in every month, and examines seasonality, between-site differences, sampling coverage and alternative measurement networks. It is descriptive research, not a regulatory compliance assessment, causal estimate or population-weighted exposure analysis.

## Data and comparison design

The comparison uses an annual observed-network average with an average over the six fixed sites. The first can change because site values change or because sites enter and leave; the second holds site membership fixed. Endpoint and multi-year-window comparisons also ask different questions.

The primary analysis uses parameter 88101 and selects one monitor per site-year. It calculates site-month means, gives sites equal weight within each month, then gives months equal weight within each year. Under this estimator, the observed-network mean changes from **10.952 to 10.802 µg/m³** (−0.149), while the fixed six-site mean changes from **11.254 to 12.064 µg/m³** (+0.811). The endpoint-change contrast is approximately **0.960 µg/m³**. Comparing 2015–2017 with 2022–2024 instead gives declines of 0.591 and 0.239 µg/m³, respectively. Site mix therefore affects apparent improvement, but the non-monotonic series does not establish a steady countywide increase or decline.

Missing-day and coverage assumptions affect the magnitude. The separate 88502 network uses different sites and measurement methods and is not pooled with 88101. Cadence-based coverage is an analyst-defined proxy, not verified scheduled-sample completeness. There is no imputation, meteorological adjustment, formal significance test or causal identification. Full findings, sensitivity results and interpretation appear in the saved Session response and bundled tables.

## What the comparison shows

The opposite endpoint changes show that monitoring-network composition matters. Declines under the multi-year comparison further show why the chosen temporal summary must accompany any trend claim.

## Assumptions and limits

These equal-site averages describe monitoring results, not population exposure or regulatory compliance. Comparing 2015–2017 with 2022–2024 gave declines in both networks; coverage assumptions and the comparison window affect the conclusion.

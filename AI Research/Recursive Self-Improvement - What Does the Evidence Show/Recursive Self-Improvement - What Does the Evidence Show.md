# Recursive Self-Improvement - What Does the Evidence Show

**Creator:** @dongxi_nlp  

## Research question

Which feedback loops are supported by evidence, and which remain proposed research programs?

Organizes a literature-based investigation of recursive self-improvement, covering methods, evidence, trends, and economic constraints. The archived research package includes a survey, a methods taxonomy, a claims-evidence matrix, bibliographic records, a chronology, a conceptual feedback diagram, reproducible build materials, and an agenda of three falsifiable experiments.

## Data and comparison design

Organize methods and claims in a literature register, link each claim to its evidence family, and separate automated optimization, tool-building, and self-modification. Keep the conceptual feedback diagram distinct from an executed recursive improvement loop.

## Recorded findings

| Evidence class | What the review records | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Survey and chronology | Organizes reported systems and their development | A chronology is not a controlled test of accelerating progress. |
| Evidence matrix | Connects each claim with its supplied support | Separates a demonstrated component from a broad autonomy claim. |
| Taxonomy | Distinguishes feedback and improvement mechanisms | Avoids treating ordinary optimization as an unrestricted recursive loop. |
| Experiment proposals | Three falsifiable proposals | Remain proposed, rather than executed findings. |

The archive provides a survey, taxonomy, evidence matrix, chronology, and three falsifiable experiment proposals. The proposals remain unexecuted.

The review distinguishes a system improving an output, improving a tool it uses, and changing a mechanism responsible for later improvement. These can all produce better results, but only the last kind of feedback bears directly on a strong recursive-improvement claim. Even then, repeated gains must be separated from extra compute, additional human intervention and an easier evaluation.

1. Build a register of the supplied works, their mechanisms and their stated claims.
2. Link claims to the evidence actually provided, distinguishing implemented components from proposed systems.
3. Place the systems in a taxonomy and chronology without treating temporal sequence as proof of acceleration.
4. Formulate experiments that could falsify the proposed mechanism and retain their unexecuted status.

The resulting evidence matrix is useful because a reader can ask what was modified, who selected it, how improvement was measured and whether the process repeated. The feedback diagram organizes those questions; it is not a record that an autonomous open-ended loop was run.

## What the comparison shows

A reviewed literature package can clarify hypotheses without demonstrating autonomous recursive self-improvement.

# Does AI Practice Translate into Independent Learning

**Creator:** @cheery9998  

## Research question

Do improvements while a tool is available survive an independent assessment after it is removed?

Examines whether improved performance during AI-assisted practice carries over to an independent assessment after AI is removed. The archived analysis distinguishes Control, GPT Base, and GPT Tutor, maps the supplied treatment encodings, excludes Honors classes from the main analysis, and summarizes practice and examination scores.

## Data and comparison design

The analysis separates the assisted practice phase from the later closed-book phase, identify treatment encodings, and distinguish a student from a student-session record. Apply the stated Honors exclusion before calculating group summaries.

The analysis separates two questions that an aggregate score can hide: whether assistance improves performance while help is present, and whether any gain remains when the student works independently. The groups, phases and unit of analysis must stay aligned. Repeated records from one student cannot automatically be counted as additional independent students.

1. Inspect treatment encodings and identify the student identifier in the supplied data.
2. Apply the stated class exclusion before forming the primary analysis sample.
3. Produce group summaries for the assisted practice and independent assessment separately.
4. Compare the direction and pattern with the publication while keeping any design and denominator differences visible.

A higher practice score alone would not establish retained learning. Conversely, differences on an independent assessment require the study’s assignment and analysis design before they can be interpreted causally. The retained output is the staged comparison and its source-paper reconciliation; numerical claims previously removed during review remain omitted.

## What the comparison shows

Raw mean differences and covariate-adjusted causal estimates must not be presented as the same result. Task assistance can improve an immediate score without demonstrating transferable learning.

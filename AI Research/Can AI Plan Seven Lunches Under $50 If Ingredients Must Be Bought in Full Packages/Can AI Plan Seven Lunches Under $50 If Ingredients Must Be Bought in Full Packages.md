# Can AI Plan Seven Lunches Under $50 If Ingredients Must Be Bought in Full Packages

## Research question

Is a seven-lunch budget feasible when groceries must be bought as whole packages?

Plans seven lunches with a fixed budget while requiring whole-package ingredient purchases and limiting recipe repeats. The archived answer includes a shopping list, serving arithmetic, and a proposed lowest-cost combination.

## Data and comparison design

The model charges for purchased packages rather than fractional ingredients. Serving requirements are summed across all seven lunches, recipe-repeat limits are enforced, and remaining contents are retained as leftovers. A cheap recipe can still require a costly first package purchase, so the optimization concerns the weekly basket under the supplied prices and capacities.

## Recorded findings

A valid lowest-cost plan is **$22.10**, leaving **$27.90** of the $50 budget.

### Seven lunches

| Day | Lunch |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Peanut carrot noodles |
| 4 | Chickpea spinach pasta |
| 5–7 | Chickpea noodle salad |

Each recipe stays within its maximum of three repeats.

### Shopping list

| Ingredient | Full package to buy | Cost |
|---|---|---:|
| Chickpeas | 4 cans | $3.60 |
| Whole-wheat pasta | 1 lb box | $2.20 |
| Noodles | 1 lb pack | $2.50 |
| Peanut butter | 16 oz jar | $3.90 |
| Carrots | 2 lb bag | $2.80 |
| Fresh spinach | 10 oz bag | $2.90 |
| Plain yogurt | 32 oz tub | $4.20 |
| **Total** | **7 packages** | **$22.10** |

The purchased packages provide enough for all seven lunches: 4 chickpea, 1 pasta, 6 noodle, 3 peanut-butter, 7 carrot, 4 spinach, and 6 yogurt servings are used. This calculation uses the supplied synthetic package prices and serving capacities.

## What the comparison shows

The arithmetic is specific to synthetic prices and recipe assumptions. Feasibility here is not a verified local shopping basket or a nutritional assessment.

## Assumptions and limits

Prices and serving capacities are supplied synthetic inputs. This demonstrates constraint accounting, not current grocery prices, nutritional adequacy, or a generally optimal meal plan.

# AI Explains Math - What Gets Lost

## Research question

When a theorem becomes a plain-language explanation, which assumptions or quantifiers stop being explicit?

Studies which conditions survive plain-English explanations of ten mathematical statements. The record progresses from source passages and frozen checklists to an exploratory comparison of collected explanations.

## Data and comparison design

The analysis builds item-level checklists from the source statements before classifying the explanations. Distinguish an omitted explicit condition from a changed mathematical scope, and distinguish definitions and conclusions from assumptions. The archive also checks when response collection and rule freezing happened.

## Recorded findings

| Checklist judgment | Recorded count | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning retained | 59 | An explanation preserves the checklist meaning, possibly implicitly. |
| Not explicit | 15 | A condition is not stated explicitly; this is not automatically a scope error. |
| Uncertain | 1 | The shrinking-map description needs expert adjudication. |
| Demonstrated scope error | 0 | None was demonstrated under the recorded rules, not a claim of universal correctness. |

The item review reports 59 meaning-retaining entries, 15 not explicitly stated, and one uncertain entry. No reviewed passage clearly demonstrated a scope error under the supplied rules; these mixed checklist categories are not an overall correctness rate.

The archive freezes 75 checklist entries for ten source mathematical statements. A checklist item may be an assumption, a definition or a conclusion, so pooling the counts as an overall correctness percentage would obscure what is being judged. The uncertain case concerns a description of shrinking maps. Evidence quotations and item-level classifications let a reader inspect whether an explanation preserves a condition implicitly, omits it explicitly, or actually changes the statement’s scope.

## What the comparison shows

This exploratory sample describes particular explanations under particular judgments. A readable paraphrase may preserve meaning implicitly, while an explicit checklist can still expose information a reader needs.

## Assumptions and limits

Checklist judgments distinguish omitted wording from demonstrated mathematical scope errors. Collection overlap, rules written after pilot responses, interpretation uncertainty, and lack of expert review limit generalization.
